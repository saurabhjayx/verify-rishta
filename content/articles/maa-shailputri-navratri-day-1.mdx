# Maa Shailputri: Why Does Navratri Begin with the Daughter of the Mountains?

Navratri begins, in the widely followed Navadurga sequence, with Maa Shailputri. Her name is commonly understood as "daughter of the mountain": shaila means mountain, and putri means daughter. She is associated with Parvati and with the Himalayan setting of the Goddess's story.

## What her imagery means

In traditional images, Shailputri rides a bull and carries a trident and lotus. These details are not merely decorative. The bull can suggest steadiness and strength, the trident is a familiar emblem of divine power, and the lotus often stands for spiritual unfolding.

Interpretations differ across devotional explanations. These symbols are best read as traditional associations rather than a single fixed meaning.

## Why begin with a mountain daughter?

One reading is that any spiritual practice needs a firm foundation. Before discipline, courage or transformation, a person needs steadiness: the willingness to begin and to remain grounded. Devotees may understand this as a blessing for a fresh start, while others focus on the Goddess's mythological identity and worship.

## Ghatasthapana and the first day

The first day is often associated with Ghatasthapana or Kalash Sthapana, the ritual installation of a sacred pot, where this is part of the household or community tradition. The timing and procedure depend on the local Hindu calendar. For the exact muhurat, use a trusted regional panchang, because location and calendar calculations matter.

![A decorated kalash with mango leaves and a coconut placed before an idol of Goddess Durga](/images/articles/maa-shailputri-navratri-day-1/kalash-altar.webp)

Families may offer flowers, light a lamp, recite prayers or begin a [Navratri fast](/navratri/why-do-people-fast-during-navratri). Practices vary, and devotion is not measured by how elaborate the arrangement looks online.

## The bottom line

Maa Shailputri's place at the beginning of Navratri gives the festival a quiet message: before seeking transformation, establish your intention. The mountain is not only a backdrop in the story; it can also be a reminder to begin with patience and steadiness.
