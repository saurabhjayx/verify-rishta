# Why Mutual Background Verification Before Marriage Is Fairer for Both Partners

In some marriage discussions, one person is asked to prove their background while the other person's claims are accepted without question. That imbalance can create resentment and turn a useful process into a power struggle. Mutual verification begins with a different idea: both people deserve clarity, dignity and a voice in the process.

## Fair does not mean identical in every detail

People have different jobs, documents and life circumstances. Fairness means using relevant, proportionate standards for comparable claims, not forcing every person through the exact same list regardless of context. A professional qualification may matter for one person; a business disclosure may matter for another.

## Agree on scope before checks begin

List the material claims to discuss, explain why they matter and agree on suitable evidence. Decide who will see the results, how sensitive data will be protected and how discrepancies will be addressed. Do not expand the scope without a clear reason or appropriate permission. Our guide on [what to verify before marriage](/marriage-verification-basics) lists the usual claims.

## Keep records and conversations separate

A record may help confirm a date or credential. It cannot measure kindness, compatibility or how a person will behave in the future. Use conversations to discuss values, expectations and unresolved questions; use verification for factual claims that can reasonably be checked.

## Make space for correction

Sources can contain errors, names can be similar, and documents can be outdated. A fair process distinguishes confirmed information from an inconsistency or an inconclusive result. Give the person a chance to respond before reaching a conclusion.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 12 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) gives a person the right to ask for their personal data to be corrected or erased.

## The bottom line

Mutual verification does not remove every risk from marriage. It can make the process more balanced, transparent and respectful. The objective is not to catch someone out; it is to help two people make a well-informed decision.
