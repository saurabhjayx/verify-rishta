# Marriage Scams and Hidden Financial Liabilities: How to Protect Yourself Before Marriage

Financial compatibility is not about finding the partner with the highest salary. It is about understanding the money life two people will share: income, debt, dependants, spending habits, family responsibilities, savings, career plans and who pays for what. When these topics are avoided, couples can enter marriage with very different assumptions. When a prospective partner uses emotional trust to ask for money or investments, the risk is far more immediate.

## Cyber fraud is rising, but it is not a marriage statistic

Citizens lost ₹22,845.73 crore to cyber fraud in 2024, up from ₹7,465.18 crore in 2023, the Home Ministry told the Lok Sabha ([IANS report](https://morungexpress.com/2268-lakh-cyber-crimes-registered-over-rs-22845-crore-lost-by-citizens-in-2024-centre)). These totals cover all cyber fraud. They are not broken down by matrimonial sites, so they must not be read as money lost through marriage scams.

What local reports do show is how these scams work. In Coimbatore, police recorded more than 50 matrimonial fraud complaints in which scammers spent months building trust before pitching an "investment", according to [The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/news/national/tn-matrimonial-fraud-complaints-rise-in-coimbatore-as-scammers-target-men-over-40-1124803) in September 2026.

## Red flags that deserve a pause

- A new match quickly asks for money, a loan, a gift card, cryptocurrency or help with an emergency.
- They promise unusually high returns and insist you use a trading app or website they chose.
- They discourage you from talking to family, your bank or anyone who might question the story.
- Their job, income, business or location changes when you ask reasonable follow-up questions.
- They ask for passwords, one-time passwords, PINs, remote access to your phone or access to your bank account.

One red flag does not prove fraud. A pattern of urgency, secrecy and money pressure is a strong reason to stop all transfers and check what is happening. See more [matrimonial fraud warning signs](/matrimonial-fraud).

## How to talk about money before marriage

### Income, work and lifestyle

Discuss current work, career plans, job stability and the lifestyle each of you expects. Where income or employment is a material claim, agree what evidence can be shared voluntarily. A salary slip contains sensitive information, so agree what is relevant and how it will be protected. Our guide to [verifying job, salary and education](/documents-and-records/verify-groom-job-salary-education) explains what can be confirmed.

### Debt, dependants and existing commitments

Ask about education loans, personal loans, business obligations, dependants, support for parents and any other major commitment that could affect the household. Having debt does not make someone a bad partner. What matters is honesty, affordability and whether both of you understand the consequences. Read how to [check financial liabilities before marriage](/documents-and-records/check-financial-liabilities-before-marriage).

### Household money and independence

Talk about joint and separate accounts, savings, emergency funds, major purchases, family support and how money decisions will be made. Both partners should keep independent access to money. No one should be pressured to hand over passwords or give up control of their finances.

### Dowry is not financial planning

Demands for dowry, forced transfers and money pressure are not wedding customs to shrug off. NCRB recorded 12,343 cases under the Dowry Prohibition Act and 5,737 dowry deaths in 2024 ([The Wire's summary of the NCRB figures](https://m.thewire.in/article/women/16-dowry-deaths-every-day-in-2024-ncrb-data-shows)). Giving, taking or demanding dowry is an offence under the [Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/8dc8e1b4-7147-45ea-9e82-5911aa3816d4/sections).

## What to do if you suspect a scam

Stop sending money, and never pay a second "fee" to recover what you lost. Call your bank or payment app at once, keep every transaction reference and message, and change any password you shared. Report it on the [National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) or call the cybercrime helpline, 1930 ([cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/)). If you are threatened or coerced, seek local support, and ask a qualified lawyer or financial adviser about your situation.

## Where verification fits

You can check claims yourselves, ask a lawyer or financial adviser, or use a consent-based verification service. [Milne Se Pehle](https://milnesepehle.in/) is one such service (see our [disclosure](/disclosure)). Depending on the service, the law and the records available, a check can cover identity, education, employment, address, references and relevant financial liabilities.

Verification can surface inconsistencies worth a conversation. It cannot guarantee honesty, prove every financial fact or replace independent financial advice.

## The bottom line

The strongest protection combines honest conversation, sensible checks, independent judgement and one firm rule: a romantic relationship is never a reason to ignore basic financial safety.
