# How to Verify Someone Before Marriage in India: A Complete Guide

Marriage is a major personal decision, and it is reasonable to want clarity about important information before committing. Verification is not about assuming that someone is dishonest or investigating them secretly. It is about checking material claims fairly, discussing expectations openly, and making decisions with better information.

## What should you verify before marriage?

Start with the claims that matter to your decision. These may include identity and age; educational qualifications; current employment and professional credentials; current address; marital status and relevant prior-marriage disclosures; and financial commitments that both people agree to discuss. The right checks depend on the claims made, the evidence available, and the applicable law.

### 1. Confirm identity and basic details

Compare the person's stated name, age, and other relevant details with documents they voluntarily provide. Check for inconsistencies rather than treating a document image or social profile as conclusive proof. Where a formal verification service is used, understand what source it checks and what result it can actually confirm.

### 2. Verify education and employment claims

A degree certificate, professional registration, offer letter, or online profile may be useful evidence, but each has limits. Where appropriate, request consent for confirmation through the issuing institution, relevant registry, or employer's authorised process. Do not impersonate the person or seek confidential records without permission. Our guide on how to [verify employment and education](/documents-and-records/verify-groom-job-salary-education) goes into more detail.

### 3. Discuss marital status and financial commitments

Ask direct, respectful questions about previous marriage, divorce, children, dependants, loans, guarantees, and other financial obligations that may affect shared plans. Documents or public records may help in some situations, but records can be incomplete, difficult to match, or unavailable. Seek qualified legal advice if a material discrepancy needs interpretation. See also how to [check for a previous marriage](/documents-and-records/verify-previous-marriage-india) and how to discuss [financial liabilities before marriage](/documents-and-records/check-financial-liabilities-before-marriage).

### 4. Understand criminal and court-record checks

A search result, complaint, FIR reference, or allegation is not the same as a conviction. Court and police information can be fragmented, and a name-only search can misidentify someone. Any record review should use lawful sources, appropriate identity matching, careful interpretation, and an opportunity to clarify discrepancies.

### 5. Keep the process mutual and consent-based

A healthy process should apply fair standards to both people. Explain what you want to verify and why, agree on reasonable documents or checks, protect copies and personal information, and avoid publishing or forwarding sensitive findings. A verification report is one input into a decision, not a guarantee about a person's character or future behaviour.

## A practical checklist

1. Write down the important claims.
2. Ask the person to confirm them.
3. Agree on suitable evidence.
4. Use official or authorised sources where possible.
5. Record what was checked and what remains unverified.
6. Discuss any discrepancy directly.
7. Get professional advice when the consequences are significant.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 3 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says it does not apply to personal data processed by an individual for a personal or domestic purpose. A family's own enquiries may fall outside it, but a verification service that handles the data is covered.
- [Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/33e4083a-e967-4091-b91f-915b4d3f95a8/sections) lists the conditions for a Hindu marriage, and the first is that neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage. [Section 4 of the Special Marriage Act, 1954](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/6b55de27-1d75-4e3d-9613-7d189ab6091e/sections) has the same condition.
- [Section 319 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/4bb3e636-ed67-4731-8fb1-7ebfd83e1584/sections) makes cheating by personation, which means pretending to be someone else, an offence. Do not pose as an employer, a relative or an official to get information.
- [Section 3 of the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/8dc8e1b4-7147-45ea-9e82-5911aa3816d4/sections) penalises giving or taking dowry, and [Section 4](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/8dc8e1b4-7147-45ea-9e82-5911aa3816d4/sections) penalises demanding it, directly or indirectly, from the family of the bride or groom.

## The bottom line

The goal is not to remove every uncertainty from marriage. It is to reduce avoidable uncertainty about important facts while respecting both people's dignity and privacy. If you use a verification service, understand the scope of its checks before relying on its results.
