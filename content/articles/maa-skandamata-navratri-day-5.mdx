# Maa Skandamata: Why Is the Mother Form Central to Navratri?

Maa Skandamata is worshipped on the fifth day of the widely followed Navadurga sequence. Her name connects her with Skanda, also known as Kartikeya, a deity associated with war and divine protection. She is commonly depicted holding her child, which makes maternal care central to her iconography.

## Tenderness and strength together

At first glance, a mother and child may seem far from the fierce warrior forms of Goddess Durga. Yet the two ideas are not opposites in the tradition. Protection can take the form of tenderness, nurture and responsibility, as well as decisive strength when danger appears.

## What her imagery means

Skandamata's imagery is often read as compassion, family care and protective power. Devotees may pray for the wellbeing of children and family members. These prayers express devotional hopes; worship does not guarantee a particular result.

A mother holding her child is recognisable across many cultures. In the Navratri setting, it adds a dimension of care to the larger story of Shakti, the divine feminine power.

## How the day is observed

Rituals and offerings differ across households. Some people observe a fast, some visit temples, and others keep a simple prayer at home. No one family's custom is the only authentic way to honour the Goddess.

## The bottom line

Skandamata reminds devotees that strength is not only the power to defeat an enemy. It is also the patience to care, the willingness to protect someone vulnerable and the responsibility to help others grow.
