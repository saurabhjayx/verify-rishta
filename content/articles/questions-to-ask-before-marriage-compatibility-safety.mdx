# Before You Get Married: 15 Questions Every Couple Should Discuss and Verify

A good premarital conversation is not an interview designed to catch someone out. It is a chance for two people to understand how they want to live, what they expect from each other and where their plans differ. Asking uncomfortable questions early is kinder than discovering after marriage that each of you assumed something completely different.

## Why preparation matters

Official figures are a reminder that some marriages run into serious trouble. Family courts received more than eight lakh new cases in 2023, according to a [Lok Sabha answer on family courts](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2974769/1/AS113.pdf), though these cover family disputes of all kinds, not only divorces. Police registered 1,20,227 cases of cruelty by a husband or his relatives in 2024 ([NCRB, Crime in India 2024](https://www.ncrb.gov.in/crime-in-india-year-wise.html?year=2024)).

These numbers do not predict anything about your relationship. They are a reason to take expectations, boundaries and safety seriously before the wedding, not after.

## Fifteen questions to discuss before marriage

### Values and the life you want

1. What does a happy marriage look like to you in everyday life?
2. Do you want children? If yes, roughly when, and what if our plans or health circumstances change?
3. What do we expect about religion, traditions, festivals and how children would be raised, if we have them?

### Work, location and family

4. Will both of us be free to work, study, change careers or take a break from work?
5. Where will we live after marriage, and how will we decide if a job needs a move?
6. Does either of us expect to live with parents, or to give ongoing care or money to family members?
7. How will we divide housework and caregiving, including when one of us is busy or unwell?

### Money and commitments

8. What do we expect about income, spending, savings and lifestyle?
9. Are there loans, dependants, business commitments or other money responsibilities that could affect our plans?
10. How will we handle joint expenses, separate accounts, emergency savings and big purchases?

### Boundaries, trust and conflict

11. What privacy do we expect around phones, social media, friendships and personal time?
12. How do we want to handle disagreements, and what behaviour is never acceptable in an argument?
13. What does fidelity mean to each of us, and what boundaries do we expect with others?
14. Are there health, safety or personal circumstances each of us should understand before a lifelong commitment? Share sensitive things thoughtfully, and seek professional guidance where it helps.
15. What would make either of us feel pressured or unsafe, and how will we respect a clear "no" or a request for more time?

For the practical side of these topics, see what [couples should verify and discuss before marriage](/marriage-verification-basics/verify-and-discuss-before-marriage).

## What to discuss, and what to verify

Conversation and verification do different jobs. A conversation reveals preferences, values and how open someone is willing to be. Verification checks a specific factual claim. If a qualification, employer, identity detail or financial commitment matters to your decision, it is reasonable to ask for proportionate evidence shared voluntarily.

A background check cannot tell you whether someone will be a considerate partner. A good conversation cannot confirm every fact. You need both.

## A practical verification checklist

- Confirm identity details through lawful methods, and collect only the documents you genuinely need.
- With consent, check material education and employment claims through official channels where available.
- Discuss income, loans, dependants and money expectations. Never ask for passwords, one-time passwords or account access.
- Meet in safe settings, tell someone you trust about your plans, and never send money to a person whose identity you have not checked.
- Watch behaviour: pressure, secrecy, threats, contempt, isolation or repeated boundary violations matter even when the documents look right.

## The bottom line

The goal is not to remove every uncertainty. It is to make sure the important decisions are not made in the dark. Ask difficult questions with kindness, answer honestly, check material facts with consent and give each other time to decide freely. A healthy "yes" never depends on pressure, secrecy or avoidable misinformation.
