# If You Have Nothing to Hide, Why Should Verification Before Marriage Bother You?

Willingness to clarify important facts can build confidence, but privacy is legitimate too. A fair process asks for relevant evidence, not unlimited access to a person's life.

## A provocative question with an important limit

"If you have nothing to hide, why fear verification?" can open a conversation, but it can also become unfair pressure. People have legitimate privacy boundaries, and declining an intrusive request is not proof of wrongdoing. The stronger principle is this: both people should be willing to discuss material claims and consider proportionate ways to confirm them.

## What reasonable verification looks like

A reasonable check is tied to a clear question. Is this qualification accurately described? Is the stated job current? Has a material marital-status fact been disclosed?

The check uses an appropriate source, collects only relevant information and explains the result and its limitations.

## What it should not become

It should not require passwords, private chats, unrestricted bank access, location tracking or access to unrelated personal history. Nor should a report be used to shame, threaten or control someone. Verification is due diligence, not ownership.

## Why mutuality matters

If one person is asked to provide evidence, the other should be willing to discuss equivalent material claims. [Mutual verification](/marriage-verification-basics/mutual-verification-before-marriage) helps avoid a dynamic where one person is treated as a suspect and the other as an unquestioned judge.

## What if someone is uncomfortable?

Ask which part feels uncomfortable: the data requested, the provider, the cost, the method or the principle. Consider alternatives, narrower checks or direct document sharing. If the parties cannot agree, they should decide whether the remaining uncertainty is acceptable rather than forcing consent.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 12 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) gives a person the right to ask for their personal data to be corrected or erased.

## The bottom line

The best question is not whether a person has "nothing to hide." It is whether both people can have an honest, fair conversation about the facts that matter, while preserving legitimate privacy and freedom of choice.
