# Why Self-Verification Is Becoming Important Before Marriage in India

Marriage introductions in India can involve families, matchmaking platforms, social networks and online profiles. Information often moves between several people before the prospective couple has a chance to speak directly. That makes accuracy and context important. Self-verification is a practical habit: review the claims you make, correct outdated details and be ready to discuss material facts.

## Profiles can become outdated

People change employers, cities, qualifications, responsibilities and relationship circumstances. An old biodata or profile may not reflect the current position. Checking your own details helps you catch mistakes before someone else has to ask about them. Families reviewing a profile can use our guide to [verify a matrimonial biodata](/for-parents/how-to-verify-matrimonial-biodata).

## Trust works better when it is mutual

A one-sided demand can feel like an accusation. A mutual agreement to confirm relevant claims creates a clearer expectation: both people can ask reasonable questions and both can explain the evidence they choose to share. This does not mean either person must surrender all privacy.

## Verification is not a substitute for compatibility

Records can help confirm certain factual claims. They cannot tell you whether you will agree on children, careers, money, care for parents, household work or conflict. These topics need thoughtful conversation. A sound process combines factual checks with compatibility discussions.

## What a responsible process looks like

- Agree on the claims to check.
- Explain the purpose.
- Obtain consent or authority where required.
- Use official or authorised sources where possible.
- Record what is confirmed and what is inconclusive.
- Keep documents secure.
- Provide a fair chance to explain discrepancies.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 3 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says it does not apply to personal data processed by an individual for a personal or domestic purpose. A family's own enquiries may fall outside it, but a verification service that handles the data is covered.

## The bottom line

Self-verification is not about proving that you are flawless. It is about taking responsibility for the information you share and making a serious decision easier for everyone involved. The strongest message is not "I have nothing to hide"; it is "Let's be clear, fair and accurate with each other."
