# What Should a Marriage Verification Report or Certificate Actually Prove?

A trustworthy report should explain the checks performed, the sources and dates, the findings and the limitations. Be cautious of any certificate promising complete certainty about a person.

## A certificate is only as useful as its scope

The word "certificate" can sound definitive, but a document cannot prove everything about a person. Before relying on a marriage verification report, understand what was actually checked, which sources were used, how identity was matched and what time period the information covers.

## What a responsible report should explain

It should identify the checks included, the date or period of each check, the evidence source or source category, the outcome and any limitations. It should distinguish verified information from self-reported details, unresolved discrepancies, unavailable records and matters not checked. It should also explain how a person can raise a correction if information appears inaccurate.

## Understand common result types

- **Verified** should refer to a defined claim and evidence standard, not a broad endorsement of character.
- **Inconsistent** means information did not match as expected and needs context.
- **Inconclusive** means the available evidence did not support a firm conclusion.
- **Not checked** means no conclusion should be inferred.

These labels should be defined by the provider.

## What a report cannot guarantee

A report cannot guarantee that a person is honest in every area, that no record exists anywhere, or that a marriage will succeed. Public records may be incomplete; names can match different people; documents may be outdated; and future behaviour cannot be predicted. Be sceptical of absolute claims.

## Privacy, consent and correction

A provider should explain how it obtains authority or consent where required, protects sensitive information, limits access, retains documents and handles correction requests. Share reports only with people who have a legitimate reason to see them. Do not use a report to shame or threaten someone.

## Questions to ask before buying

- What exact checks are included, and what is excluded?
- Which sources are used?
- How are identity matches confirmed?
- What happens if a source is unavailable?
- Can the person respond to a discrepancy?
- What is the data retention policy?
- What is the total cost?
- Are any results guaranteed that the provider cannot reasonably guarantee?

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 8 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) requires an organisation to keep personal data complete, accurate and consistent when it is used to make a decision about that person, and to protect it with reasonable security safeguards.
- [Section 12 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) gives a person the right to ask for their personal data to be corrected or erased.

## The bottom line

A credible report gives you a transparent account of selected checks, not a magical verdict on a human being. Read the scope, understand uncertainty and use the findings alongside direct conversation and judgment.
