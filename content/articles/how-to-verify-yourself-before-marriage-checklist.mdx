# How to Verify Yourself Before Marriage: A Practical Checklist

Before asking a prospective partner to verify their claims, review your own profile. This checklist helps you correct outdated information and prepare evidence responsibly. It is the practical side of [self-verification before marriage](/detective-vs-self-verification/self-verification-before-marriage).

## Step 1: Review your profile

Read your matrimonial biodata and online profiles as if you were seeing them for the first time. Check names, dates, education, employment, location and family details for errors or outdated claims. Correct anything that could reasonably mislead someone. Our [matrimonial biodata checklist](/for-parents/how-to-verify-matrimonial-biodata) shows what the other family is likely to look at.

## Step 2: Check education and professional claims

Confirm the exact qualification, institution name, completion status and dates. If you hold a regulated professional credential, check that your registration is current through the appropriate official channel. Do not describe a short course as a degree or an incomplete qualification as completed.

## Step 3: Clarify employment and income

Check your current job title, employer, employment status and dates. Describe compensation accurately, distinguishing fixed salary from variable pay or total compensation. If self-employed, avoid presenting business turnover as personal take-home income.

## Step 4: Prepare for material conversations

Think through information that may materially affect a shared future, such as prior marriage, children, significant financial obligations, dependants, relocation or caregiving responsibilities. The level of disclosure should be relevant and respectful, not a demand to reveal every private detail.

## Step 5: Organise evidence safely

Keep relevant documents in a secure place. Share only what is necessary, mask unrelated identifiers where appropriate, and avoid sending passwords or one-time codes. If an institution needs to confirm a claim, use its authorised process and consent requirements.

## Step 6: Be honest about uncertainty

If a document is unavailable or a record cannot be confirmed, say so. Do not create a misleading certificate or claim that a check happened when it did not. A useful checklist separates confirmed, self-reported, inconclusive and unchecked items.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 12 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) gives a person the right to ask for their personal data to be corrected or erased.
- [Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/33e4083a-e967-4091-b91f-915b4d3f95a8/sections) lists the conditions for a Hindu marriage, and the first is that neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage. [Section 4 of the Special Marriage Act, 1954](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/6b55de27-1d75-4e3d-9613-7d189ab6091e/sections) has the same condition.

## The bottom line

Self-verification is a practical act of preparation. It gives you the chance to correct mistakes before they become misunderstandings and helps set a fair standard for both people.
