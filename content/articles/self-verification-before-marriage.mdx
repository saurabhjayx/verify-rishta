# Self-Verification Before Marriage in India: Why It Should Be a Green Flag

Marriage discussions often ask people to prove that they are suitable partners. A more balanced approach is to invite both people to be clear about the facts that matter. Voluntarily sharing evidence for relevant claims can signal that you take the decision seriously and are comfortable having an honest conversation.

## Why self-verification can be a green flag

First, it shows preparation: you have checked that your own documents and claims are consistent. Second, it sets a mutual standard rather than placing one person under suspicion. Third, it can surface errors early, such as outdated employment details or a qualification that is described imprecisely. Finally, it makes the conversation more practical: both people can discuss what has been confirmed and what remains personal.

## What could you verify about yourself?

Depending on what you have represented and what is relevant, review your identity details, educational credentials, professional registrations, employment dates and job title, current address claims, and any material marital-status disclosure. If you choose to discuss finances, use current and appropriately limited evidence. Do not collect or share documents simply to create the appearance of a perfect profile. Our [self-verification checklist](/documents-and-records/how-to-verify-yourself-before-marriage-checklist) walks through each step.

## A self-check is not a claim of perfection

A self-verification process should not be marketed as a guarantee that every statement about you is true or that no issue could ever arise. It records specific checks, their sources and their dates. Some facts may remain unverified because a source is unavailable or will not respond. State those limits clearly.

## How to offer it without sounding defensive

You might say: "I believe both people should feel comfortable before making this decision. I'm happy to clarify the important details I've shared and discuss a fair verification process that applies to both of us." This frames verification as shared due diligence, not a challenge or accusation.

## Protect your own information

Share only information relevant to the agreed purpose. Mask unnecessary account numbers and identifiers, use secure channels, and ask how copies will be stored or deleted. Never send passwords, one-time passcodes or access to private accounts. A credible process should explain why each document is needed.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 12 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) gives a person the right to ask for their personal data to be corrected or erased.

## The bottom line

Self-verification will not replace conversation, compatibility or judgment. But a willingness to confirm material claims, acknowledge uncertainty and apply fair standards can make a difficult discussion easier. Trust the person, and be willing to verify the facts together.
