# Matrimonial Fraud in India: How to Verify a Marriage Prospect Before Saying Yes

A matrimonial profile can look convincing, a family introduction can feel reassuring and a long conversation can create a sense of closeness. None of these, on its own, proves that every important claim is true. The safest approach is neither blind trust nor intrusive surveillance: ask direct questions, check material facts with consent, and never let pressure replace judgement.

## What the numbers show, and what they do not

Cyber fraud is growing fast in India. Citizens lost ₹22,845.73 crore to cyber fraud in 2024, up from ₹7,465.18 crore in 2023, the Home Ministry told the Lok Sabha ([IANS report](https://morungexpress.com/2268-lakh-cyber-crimes-registered-over-rs-22845-crore-lost-by-citizens-in-2024-centre)). These are totals for all cyber fraud. They are not broken down by matrimonial sites, so no national figure shows how much of it began with a marriage profile.

Local reports show the pattern is real. In Coimbatore, police recorded more than 50 matrimonial fraud complaints, mostly from unmarried men over 40, according to [The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/news/national/tn-matrimonial-fraud-complaints-rise-in-coimbatore-as-scammers-target-men-over-40-1124803) in September 2026. The scammers built trust over months of calls and messages, then pitched an "investment".

Scams target people of every gender, age and income. Scammers tailor the story to the person they want to persuade. That is a reason for everyone to be careful, not a reason to treat any group as suspicious.

## How matrimonial scams usually work

A common pattern starts with a fake or misleading identity and a fast push for emotional closeness. Then comes a financial emergency, a request for a transfer, or an invitation to a supposedly profitable investment or crypto platform. For more signs to watch for, read our guide to [matrimonial fraud warning signs](/matrimonial-fraud).

Other warning signs:

- Avoiding video calls or meeting in person.
- Life details that change when you ask follow-up questions.
- Pressure to keep the relationship secret from family.
- Requests for a one-time password, PIN or access to your phone.
- Any request for money before you have met and checked who they are.

## A safer verification checklist

### Confirm identity and consistency

Ask for the person's full name and the basic details that matter for the marriage. Compare documents they share voluntarily for reasonable consistency. Do not collect or forward identity documents you do not need, and never use another person's login or credentials. Our [biodata checklist](/for-parents/how-to-verify-matrimonial-biodata) shows which details to check first.

### Verify education and employment

Ask about qualifications, the institution, the current role and work history. With clear permission, use the institution's or employer's official channels where they exist. A business card, a social media page or an email signature is useful context, but none of them is proof on its own.

### Discuss money openly

Talk about income, loans, dependants, financial commitments and how household costs will be shared. If a financial claim matters to the decision, agree what evidence can be shared voluntarily. Never ask for or give passwords, one-time passwords or account access.

### Treat any money request as a warning

Do not send money to someone whose identity you have not checked, and do not invest through a platform a romantic prospect introduced. If you have already paid and suspect fraud, call your bank immediately and report it on the [National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) or the cybercrime helpline, 1930 ([cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/)). Read more about [protecting your money before marriage](/matrimonial-fraud/marriage-scams-hidden-financial-liabilities-before-marriage).

### Keep the process calm and fair

Explain why a check matters, what information you need and how you will protect it. Give the other person a chance to explain a mismatch. A refusal to share something private is not automatic proof of fraud; look at the whole pattern, including whether their story holds together and whether they respect your boundaries.

## The bottom line

Do not confuse familiarity with verification. Take your time, confirm the claims that matter, protect your data, keep money out of a relationship you have not checked, and treat pressure as a reason to slow down.
