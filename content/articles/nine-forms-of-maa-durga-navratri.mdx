# Who Are the Nine Forms of Maa Durga? Navadurga Names and Meaning

The nine forms honoured during many Navratri observances are collectively called the Navadurga. In devotional practice, each day is associated with one form of the Goddess. The sequence is familiar to many households, but the symbolism behind the names is less widely understood.

## The nine forms, day by day

In the widely followed order, one form is worshipped on each of the nine days:

1. **[Maa Shailputri](/navratri/maa-shailputri-navratri-day-1).** Her name means daughter of the mountain. She is commonly shown riding a bull and holding a trident and lotus. Devotees associate her with groundedness, nature and a strong beginning.
2. **[Maa Brahmacharini](/navratri/maa-brahmacharini-navratri-day-2).** This form is linked with penance, discipline and devotion. She is commonly depicted carrying a rosary and a water pot, representing spiritual practice and restraint.
3. **[Maa Chandraghanta](/navratri/maa-chandraghanta-navratri-day-3).** Named for the crescent-shaped bell associated with her forehead, she is often depicted as a warrior form. Her imagery suggests courage, alertness and protection.
4. **[Maa Kushmanda](/navratri/maa-kushmanda-navratri-day-4).** In popular devotional explanations, her name is associated with the cosmic egg and the creation or illumination of the universe. She represents creative energy and radiance.
5. **[Maa Skandamata](/navratri/maa-skandamata-navratri-day-5).** Revered as the mother of Skanda, or Kartikeya, she is associated with maternal care, protection and compassion.
6. **[Maa Katyayani](/navratri/maa-katyayani-navratri-day-6).** A fierce form of the Goddess, she is linked in tradition with the sage Katyayana and the defeat of demonic forces. Devotees associate her with courage and decisive action.
7. **[Maa Kalaratri](/navratri/maa-kalaratri-navratri-day-7).** Her formidable appearance represents the power that confronts darkness and fear. Despite her fierce iconography, devotees also regard her as protective and auspicious.
8. **[Maa Mahagauri](/navratri/maa-mahagauri-navratri-day-8).** Often depicted in white, she is associated with purity, peace and serenity.
9. **[Maa Siddhidatri](/navratri/maa-siddhidatri-navratri-day-9).** Her name is commonly interpreted as the giver of siddhis, or spiritual attainments. She represents fulfilment, wisdom and the close of the nine-day sequence.

## Nine goddesses, or one?

In the Navadurga tradition, the nine are understood as forms or manifestations of one Goddess rather than nine unrelated deities. Each form brings a different quality into focus, from steadiness and discipline to courage, care and fulfilment. How devotees explain that relationship can differ by tradition.

## Details differ by region and family

Details such as colours, offerings, mantras and ritual procedures can differ by regional calendar, family tradition and source. Popular online [Navratri colour charts](/navratri/navratri-colours-meaning-and-tradition) are best understood as a practical custom rather than a universal scriptural rule. The central idea is the worship of the Goddess through distinct forms and qualities.

## The bottom line

The Navadurga gives each night of Navratri its own character. Knowing what each form represents makes the sequence more than a list of names.
