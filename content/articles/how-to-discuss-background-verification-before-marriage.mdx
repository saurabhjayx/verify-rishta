# How to Talk to a Prospective Partner About Background Verification Without Hurting Trust

The way you introduce verification matters. Start with shared goals, explain the specific questions you want to clarify and invite the other person to shape a fair process.

## Start with your shared goal

Choose a calm moment, not an argument. Explain that marriage is a major commitment and you want both people to feel comfortable with the important facts. Avoid implying that you have already decided the person is dishonest.

## A conversation starter you can use

"I want us both to feel confident before taking the next step. Would you be comfortable discussing the important details we've shared and agreeing on a fair way to verify any material claims? I'm happy for the same standard to apply to me."

## Be specific, not vague

"I want to check everything about you" sounds invasive. Instead, name the question: current employment, a professional qualification, a material marital-status disclosure or a financial obligation relevant to shared plans. Explain why it matters and ask what evidence the person is comfortable sharing.

## Listen to the concern

The other person may worry about privacy, data security, cost, timing or being judged by their family. Take the concern seriously. Narrow the scope, discuss alternatives, and explain how documents will be protected. Consent should not be obtained through threats, humiliation or emotional pressure.

## If there is disagreement

A refusal is not proof of deception. Ask whether the concern is about the particular information, the method or the provider. If no acceptable method exists, decide whether the remaining uncertainty is something you can live with. Each person must remain free to make their own decision.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 6 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, and limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose.
- [Section 3 of the DPDP Act](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/c058fa9f-eaf0-4ca3-98f1-3443b087bca9/sections) says it does not apply to personal data processed by an individual for a personal or domestic purpose. A family's own enquiries may fall outside it, but a verification service that handles the data is covered.

## The bottom line

A good verification conversation is direct, mutual and respectful. The goal is clarity before commitment, not catching someone in a mistake.
