# How to Verify a Matrimonial Biodata: 12 Things to Check Before Saying Yes

A matrimonial biodata is a starting point for conversation, not proof that every claim is accurate. Whether a profile comes through family, a matchmaking platform, or a friend, use the same calm process. Identify the claims that matter, ask respectful questions, and seek appropriate evidence when needed.

## The 12-point checklist

1. **Full name and age:** confirm that basic details are consistent across the information voluntarily shared.
2. **Education:** ask about the institution, qualification and year; verify through appropriate channels when material.
3. **Employment:** clarify employer, role, work location and employment status.
4. **Income:** distinguish fixed salary, variable pay, business revenue and take-home pay.
5. **Professional credentials:** check relevant registrations where the occupation requires them.
6. **Current location:** clarify residence and relocation plans.
7. **Family details:** discuss the information that affects your future plans without making assumptions about family background.
8. **Marital status:** ask directly about previous marriage, divorce, children or other material disclosures.
9. **Financial commitments:** discuss loans, dependants and obligations that could affect shared finances.
10. **Lifestyle and expectations:** talk about work, caregiving, children, household responsibilities and personal boundaries.
11. **Online consistency:** compare public professional claims carefully, but remember profiles can be outdated or impersonated.
12. **Documents and evidence:** note what has actually been confirmed and what remains self-reported.

For job and education claims in particular, see how to [verify job, salary and education](/documents-and-records/verify-groom-job-salary-education).

## Look for discrepancies, not stereotypes

A discrepancy can have an innocent explanation: a job change, an old profile, a name variation, or a difference between gross and take-home pay. Avoid treating community rumours, anonymous posts, appearance, caste, income bracket, or social status as evidence of character. Ask for clarification and assess reliable evidence instead.

## How to verify responsibly

Explain why a claim matters to you and ask whether the person is comfortable providing relevant evidence or authorising a check. Use official or authorised sources where possible. Do not access private accounts, impersonate someone, or circulate documents. If the issue has legal significance, consult a qualified professional rather than relying on social media conclusions.

## A simple evidence tracker

For each material claim, note the claim, the evidence offered, the source checked, the date, the outcome (confirmed, inconsistent, inconclusive or not checked), and the next question. This helps prevent assumptions from being mistaken for facts.

## What the law says

The provisions below are summarised from the official text on India Code. Laws and their commencement change, so check the current text or ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

- [Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/33e4083a-e967-4091-b91f-915b4d3f95a8/sections) lists the conditions for a Hindu marriage, and the first is that neither party has a spouse living at the time of the marriage. [Section 4 of the Special Marriage Act, 1954](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/6b55de27-1d75-4e3d-9613-7d189ab6091e/sections) has the same condition.
- [Section 69 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/4bb3e636-ed67-4731-8fb1-7ebfd83e1584/sections) covers sexual intercourse obtained by deceitful means or a false promise to marry, and its explanation includes marrying by suppressing identity.
- [Section 318 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/4bb3e636-ed67-4731-8fb1-7ebfd83e1584/sections) defines cheating, and [Section 319](https://indiacode.gov.in/act/4bb3e636-ed67-4731-8fb1-7ebfd83e1584/sections) covers cheating by personation, which means pretending to be someone else.

## The bottom line

A biodata can help two families begin a conversation, but a careful decision requires discussion and proportionate verification. Keep the process mutual, transparent and respectful. A structured service can help organise selected checks, but no report replaces direct communication and personal judgment.
